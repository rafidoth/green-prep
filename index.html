4. Chapter 2
2.1 Why saving money is green
  • Profit = revenues − costs.
  • Saving money may be more powerful than making money.
  • Cost savings continue to repeat year after year.
  • Cost cutting is usually green.
  • Expenditures involve resource use, generate waste products, and have a carbon footprint.
  • Megawatts: energy savings are measured in megawatts.
2.2 Getting focused on money-saving efforts
  1. The cost of cost-cutting efforts is more predictable than the cost of revenue-generating efforts.
  2. The savings of cost-cutting efforts are more predictable than the revenues from revenue-generating efforts.
  3. A cost-cutting effort usually pays off year after year after year.
2.3 Implementing energy efficiency
  1. Changing how current devices are used
  2. Buying new devices that are similar to current devices but more energy efficient
  3. Buying new devices that are a different class of devices

  • Turn off end-user computing devices (monitors, computers, printers) when not in use.
  • Automatic power management.
  • User education is important.
  • Policy can also help.
  • Green computing + employee health.
  • Servers: spin up, spin down
    • Specialized software and hardware
    • More complexity → more failures
2.5 Moving to cloud services
  • Cloud providers can be more efficient.
  • Cost and environmental impact go off to the provider.
  • Cloud = automatic green? Depends on efficiency, source of electricity, old infrastructure management.
  • Cloud computing is also a money saver (server, electricity, cooling, maintenance, IT staff, data center space).

5. Chapter 3
3.2 What drives the green agenda?

Greens: People who strongly advocate for environmental protection, conservation, and sustainability.

  • Traditional Stereotype: Business leaders historically assumed greens were "anti-business" or opposed to economic growth.
  • Core Philosophy: Protecting nature and ecosystems is more important than maximizing short-term quarterly profits.
  • Guiding Principle: Economic development must be sustainable and must not destroy the natural environment.
  • Modern Reality: The perception is changing—sustainable practices are now recognized as drivers of long-term business profit.
3.3 Key roots of environmentalism

The historical evolution of modern environmentalism developed in clear stages:

  1. Conservation (The Starting Point): Preserving natural resources—protecting forests, rivers, wildlife, and creating national parks.
  2. Pollution Awareness: Rising public concern over industrial air, toxic water, and chemical soil pollution.
  3. Species Protection: Enacting legal protections for endangered wildlife and plant species.
  4. Environmental Organizations: Formation of influential global citizen groups and NGOs (e.g., Greenpeace, WWF).
  5. Political Influence: Environmentalism entering government politics and lawmaking (e.g., the Green Party of Germany).
  6. Sustainability Core: Transition to the principle of meeting present needs without compromising future generations.
  7. Climate Change Priority: Global warming emerging as the central, urgent challenge of modern environmentalism.
3.4 Environmentalism and IT ⭐⭐ Common in PYQ

IT's Dual Role: Information Technology is simultaneously part of the solution and part of the environmental problem.

  • Why IT was seen as green: Enabled paperless digital workflows, virtual teleconferencing, and remote coordination; environmental groups rely heavily on technology (websites, social media, scientific modeling).
  • ⭐How IT creates environmental problems (4 Core Vectors — High Exam Yield):
    • 1. Mining rare materials: Manufacturing processors and components requires heavy mining of rare-earth metals, causing severe land destruction, toxic pollution, and high embodied carbon.
    • 2. Electricity consumption: Always-on computers, servers, high-performance labs, and data centers consume massive grid electricity, driving greenhouse gas emissions.
    • 3. Electronic waste (E-waste): Rapid hardware turnover leads to discarded devices that leach toxic heavy metals (lead, cadmium, mercury) and non-biodegradable plastics into landfills.
    • 4. Worker welfare: Informal, unregulated e-waste handling and factory assembly expose overseas workers to hazardous chemical conditions without protection.
3.5 The new imperative of climate change ⭐ PYQ Topic
  • Primary Driver: Climate change has become the single biggest driver of green computing initiatives.

Four levels through which climate change impacts an organization:

  1. Laws and regulations (Mandatory): Government emissions caps, energy standards, and recycling mandates that businesses must obey.
  2. Company policies and public statements: Internal corporate sustainability pledges, codes of conduct, and public carbon targets.
  3. Personal beliefs: Environmental values and concerns held by individual employees, managers, and customers.
  4. Strategic market positioning: How a company positions itself publicly to build customer loyalty, attract green investment, and protect reputation.
  • ⭐Professional Responsibility vs. Personal Beliefs: Regardless of personal opinions on climate science, computer engineers hold a strict professional duty to design efficient, compliant, and sustainable systems.
3.6 A brief history of the climate

Natural Climate History: Earth's climate has constantly changed over geological epochs:

  • Snowball Earth (650 million years ago): Planet was almost entirely covered in ice.
  • Warm Eras: Earth was once ~10°C warmer with significantly higher sea levels.
  • Ice Ages: Repeated natural cycles of glacial expansion and retreat.
  • Holocene: The stable, warm geological period of the last 12,000 years that enabled human agriculture and civilization.

Why is today's climate change different and dangerous?

  • Unprecedented Speed: Past changes took thousands of years; today's warming occurs in just ~100 years, leaving species and human systems little time to adapt.
  • ⭐Littoral Zones in Danger: Rapid sea level rise directly threatens coastal and delta regions where large human populations live.

Key Geological Terms:

  • Anthropocene: The current unofficial epoch where human activities have become the dominant influence on Earth's climate and ecosystems.
  • Geoengineering: Large-scale technological interventions designed to intentionally alter Earth's climate (e.g., solar radiation management, carbon removal).
3.7 Al Gore and climate change (class-9) ⭐ PYQ Topic
  • Public Advocacy: Al Gore served as a prominent global figure raising mass awareness of climate science and global warming.
  • ⭐IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change): The international United Nations scientific body that assesses peer-reviewed climate research and guides global policy.
  • Why didn't governments simply solve climate change?
    • The "Global Coordination" Problem: Greenhouse gases mix globally. One country acting alone bears the local economic cost, while polluting nations free-ride on the benefits.
    • Need for International Treaties: Coordinated international agreements (e.g., Paris Agreement) are essential to enforce shared commitments.
    • Business Collective Action Problem: Individual firms hesitate to invest in green measures if competitors continue using cheap, polluting alternatives without penalty.
3.8 The 2°C warming "limit" ⭐⭐⭐ Top PYQ Favorite

PPM: Parts Per Million (measurement of gas concentration in the atmosphere).

PeriodAtmospheric CO₂ LevelContext
Pre-industrial280 ppmNatural baseline for human civilization
Book era / Recent390+ ppmSurpassed safe limit; increases by 3 ppm each year
Doubling point560 ppmDouble pre-industrial; guarantees severe planetary crisis
  • The 2°C Rule: Internationally recognized ceiling set by science to prevent catastrophic tipping points.
  • ⭐Global Target: Keeping warming below 2°C requires cutting global emissions by 80% by 2050.
  • ⭐Five Primary Physical Consequences:
    1. Higher temperatures: Severe, prolonged heatwaves worldwide.
    2. Ice melting: Rapid thaw of polar ice sheets and mountain glaciers.
    3. Sea level rise: Submersion of coastal and littoral zones, causing climate migration.
    4. Loss of biodiversity: Mass extinction as species fail to adapt to 100-year heating.
    5. Ocean acidification: CO₂ dissolves into carbonic acid, destroying coral reefs and marine food chains.
  • Why is reducing CO₂ so difficult?
    • Economic growth conflict: Modern economic output is historically linked to fossil fuel consumption.
    • Quality of life concerns: Fears that energy cuts could lower living standards and restrict consumption.
    • Developed vs. Developing divide: Developed nations caused historical emissions, but developing nations need energy access to reduce poverty.
3.9 Climate change and IT ⭐ PYQ Topic
  • ⭐IT's Global Emissions Footprint: According to Gartner, IT generates approximately 3% of worldwide carbon emissions (growing toward 8% with cloud, crypto mining, and AI).
  • Reputational Driver: Adopting green computing directly improves IT's public reputation and corporate social responsibility (CSR) standing.
  • Two Layers of Environmental Impact:
    • Direct Impact: Emissions from manufacturing hardware, running data centers, electricity use, and e-waste disposal.
    • Indirect Impact: The systemic effects of using IT (enabling remote efficiency vs. triggering rebound consumption).
  • Core Solution: Use IT intelligently and efficiently through virtualization, power management, and workload optimization.
3.10 What next with climate change? ⭐⭐⭐ Top PYQ Favorite

Ten Projected Consequences of Exceeding ("Blowing Through") the 2°C Limit:

  1. Breaching the 2°C Threshold: Failure of international climate coordination to cap emissions.
  2. ⭐Rapid Runaway Warming (4°C–6°C): Positive feedback loops drive temperatures up by 4°C to 6°C this century.
  3. Faster Sea Level Rise: Accelerated polar ice melting rapidly inundates low-lying coastal regions.
  4. ⭐"A Great Die-Off in the Natural World": Mass extinction of wild animal and plant species unable to adapt.
  5. ⭐Acute Food and Water Shortages (2060–2069): Severe agricultural yield collapses, desertification, and glacial freshwater depletion peaking between 2060 and 2069.
  6. Surge in Fossil Fuel Use: Deepening emissions from increased coal burning, $2\times$ oil consumption, and $3\times$ natural gas use.
  7. Escalation in Extreme Weather: Unprecedented spikes in high-category cyclones, flash floods, megadroughts, and wildfires.
  8. Unconventional Fossil Fuel Extraction: Greater exploitation of environmentally damaging sources (tar sands, deep-sea drilling, fracking).
  9. Public Defeatism & Hopelessness: Widespread psychological defeatism hindering proactive climate action.
  10. Urgent Warning (IPCC Reports): International scientific assessments stress that delayed action causes irreversible damage.
3.11 What it means to "go green" ⭐ PYQ Topic
  • ⭐Triple Bottom Line (The 3 P's): Balancing People (social equity & health), Planet (environmental protection), and Profit (financial viability).
  • Traditional vs. Green Business:
    • Traditional: Focuses solely on short-term quarterly profit at the expense of resource depletion.
    • Green Business: Focuses on sustainability—not prioritizing profit first actually leads to greater long-term profit through efficiency, risk reduction, and brand loyalty.

⭐Five-Step Process for Going Green (Easy to Memorize):

  1. Think Sustainability: Identify where your operations impact the environment and where resources are wasted.
  2. Analyze Business Profits: Calculate costs, expected energy savings, and return on investment (ROI).
  3. Sort Ideas into 3 Buckets: Categorize initiatives into Yes (do immediately), Maybe (further study needed), and No (not viable).
  4. Take Action: Execute high-priority "Yes" projects without delay.
  5. Measure and Report: Track carbon and cost metrics, and share verified progress with stakeholders.
3.12 Why IT is a climate change solution ⭐⭐ Common in PYQ

IT provides two unique structural mechanisms to solve climate change (Crucial Exam Concept):

  • ⭐1. Greenability (Making IT Itself Green):
    • Computing workloads are fungible (flexible and movable).
    • Unlike traditional manufacturing factories, data centers and computing jobs can be dynamically shifted across regions to absorb intermittent renewable energy (solar and wind) when power generation peaks.
  • ⭐2. Substitutability (Using IT to Green Other Industries):
    • IT can replace high-carbon physical activities with low-carbon digital alternatives:
      • Teleconferencing & Remote Work: Replaces high-emission business travel and daily commutes with video conferencing.
      • Paperless Dematerialization: Replaces paper, physical shipping, and storage with electronic documents and cloud records.
      • Smart Energy Optimization: Uses IoT and AI to optimize energy efficiency in transportation, factories, and commercial buildings.
3.13 Green development and "going green"
  • Employer Appeal: Green computing skills make technology graduates and engineers highly attractive to modern employers.
  • Demonstrating Competence: Leading green projects demonstrates systems-level thinking, cost-saving ability, and practical leadership beyond mere environmental awareness.
  • Sustainable Career: Building expertise in energy-efficient software, cloud architecture, and carbon reduction future-proofs your engineering career.

8. Chapter 9
9.1 Why greenhouse gas emissions are important
  • Major cause of global warming.
  • IT contributes through the energy required to manufacture and operate electronic devices.
  • Greenhouse gases are gases in the atmosphere that trap heat, e.g. CO₂, CH₄.
  • Energy vs GHG emissions:
    • Energy consumption = how much electricity / fuel is used.
    • GHG emissions = how much greenhouse gas is released as a result.
  • Carbon emissions = energy consumed × emission factor.
9.2 Sources of greenhouse gases and warming

Natural: volcanic eruptions, fires, decomposition, respiration and digestion.

Human: burning coal; burning oil (including diesel and gasoline); burning natural gas; fracking for gas.

9.3 Is there still doubt about climate change?
  • Yes, there are doubters, but not significant doubt in mainstream climate science.
  • Some people question or reject aspects of climate change.
  • We should respect scientific evidence when evaluating environmental problems.
  • Facts: strong scientific evidence.
  • Solutions / policies: reasonable debate.
9.4 Why are there still doubters and deniers?
  • People don't like facts that may lead to uncomfortable consequences (psychological resistance).
  • Being skeptical is not automatically bad; sometimes it means asking for evidence and testing claims.
  • Disinformation campaigns create public uncertainty.
  • Public opinion can be influenced not only by scientific evidence but also by economic interests, political communication, and media.
  • Echo chamber: an environment where people mostly encounter information that agrees with what they already believe.

Many reasons to do green computing, believing in it or not:

  1. Saving money
  2. Reducing business risk
  3. Cutting pollution
  4. Treating people better
  5. Attracting and retaining customers, employees and supply-chain partners
  6. Complying with current and likely future regulations
  7. Positive publicity for the IT group and company
9.6 What's next with climate change? (class-14)
  • Climate change news tracking weather extremes.
  • A steady increase in events related to climate change.
  • Gradual decrease in doubt and denial as facts accumulate.
  • Climate change becoming "normal".
  • Disproportionate rewards to early adopters.
9.7 Reducing emissions I: embodied energy
  • Energy used to manufacture, transport, and build a product before it is used is called embodied energy.
  • Reducing embodied energy gives double benefits:
    1. Lower manufacturing or construction emissions
    2. Lower operating emissions
  • Example: tablet vs laptop (embodied energy ⅓, 90% less operating emissions).
  • Data center trade-off: a new building needs embodied energy, but long-term energy savings may be much greater.
9.8 Reducing emissions II: daily energy use (class-15)
  • Reducing electricity use generally reduces the greenhouse gas footprint, but it also depends on the source of electricity.
SourceNotes
CoalCheap but high carbon emission.
OilSmall but declining source of electricity; more valuable for transportation, petrochemical products etc.
Natural gasLower carbon footprint than coal (¼) and oil (½), but fracking can cause environmental problems.
HydroelectricRenewable, cheap, low emission. New dams can cause environmental damage.
Solar and windRenewable, low emission, increasing steadily.
9.9 Reducing emissions III: using different sources
  • Use cleaner energy (reduce dependency on coal); get more natural gas and renewable energy.
  • Reduce peak energy demand.
  • Use solar energy.
  • Solar thermal (sunlight to heat).
