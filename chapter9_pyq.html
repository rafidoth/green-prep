Question 1: Embodied Energy & Overall GHG Emissions Reduction

Spring 2025 – Q7 5 Marks Syllabus: Section 9.7
Original Exam Question [Spring 2025, Question 7 – CO1 – 5 Marks]

What is Embodied Energy? Discuss how understanding and reducing a product's embodied energy can be a key strategy for lowering its overall greenhouse gas emissions.

1. Definition of Embodied Energy (Section 9.7)

  • Embodied Energy Definition: The total cumulative non-renewable energy consumed across a product's entire pre-use lifecycle:
    • Raw material mining (silicon, copper, rare-earths)
    • Chemical refining and ultra-pure gas processing
    • Semiconductor component fabrication (microchip etching and cleanrooms)
    • Factory assembly and testing
    • Worldwide shipping, freight logistics, and distribution

2. How Reducing Embodied Energy Lowers Overall GHG Emissions (The Double Benefit)

  • The Double Environmental Benefit (Section 9.7):
    • 1. Lower Upfront Manufacturing Emissions: Prevents heavy industrial fossil fuel combustion during mineral extraction and semiconductor chip fabrication.
    • 2. Lower Operational Lifetime Emissions: Hardware engineered with lower physical material mass and smaller form factors inherently draws significantly less daily electricity throughout its operational lifespan.
  • Concrete Real-World Comparison (Device Classes):
    • A modern tablet consumes only ⅓ of the embodied manufacturing energy of a traditional desktop tower PC.
    • That same tablet emits 90% fewer operational emissions during daily use compared to the desktop PC.
Device Class Embodied Energy Mass Operational Electricity Draw Overall Lifecycle Emissions Impact
Traditional Desktop PC Baseline ($100\%$ Embodied Energy) High ($150\text{W}–250\text{W}$ power supply) Heaviest lifecycle carbon footprint
Energy-Efficient Laptop Moderate ($\sim 50\%$ of Desktop) Low ($45\text{W}–65\text{W}$ adapter) Substantial reduction in both phases
Modern Mobile Tablet ⅓ of Desktop embodied energy 90% fewer operating emissions ($10\text{W}–15\text{W}$) Maximum Double Environmental Benefit

3. Key Actionable Strategies to Reduce Embodied Energy

  • Hardware Lifecycle Extension:
    • Extend enterprise computer replacement cycles from 3 years to 5–6 years through RAM and solid-state drive (SSD) upgrades.
    • Spreading the fixed upfront manufacturing carbon over 6 years instead of 3 cuts annual embodied carbon by 50%.
  • Procuring Refurbished & Remanufactured Hardware:
    • Reusing existing chassis, copper wiring, and power supply units completely avoids the carbon cost of manufacturing new hardware from raw earth.
  • Right-Sizing Hardware Form Factors:
    • Deploy lightweight thin clients and virtual desktops rather than oversized workstation towers for standard clerical workers.
💡 Exam Memorization Key: Always remember the two pillars of Section 9.7: (1) Definition (pre-use lifecycle: mining → refining → fabrication → assembly → shipping) and (2) The Double Benefit (lower manufacturing footprint + lower daily operational footprint) illustrated by the Tablet vs. Desktop example.

Question 2: Enterprise Pilot Proposal for Data Center & Branch Office Decarbonization

Summer 2025 – Q5 5 Marks Syllabus: Sections 9.7, 9.8, 9.10
Original Exam Question [Summer 2025, Question 5 – 5 Marks]

A tech company operates a regional data-center and some remote branch offices. They discover that the vast majority of their IT energy use comes from equipment running 24/7 (even when idle) and the cooling systems in the data-center are outdated. A recent internal survey shows that the IT division accounts for 30% of the company’s total emissions (electricity use + upstream hardware manufacturing). The VP of IT wants you to propose a pilot project that targets both Energy Consumption and Greenhouse Gas Emissions reduction. Explain your proposal in detail.

Pilot Project Proposal: Enterprise Energy & GHG Decarbonization Initiative

1. Target Area 1: Remote Branch Offices (Daily Operational Energy – Section 9.8)

  • Centralized Automated PC Power Management:
    • Deploy Active Directory group policies enforcing deep sleep mode after 15 minutes of user inactivity across all desktop computers, displays, and printers.
    • Enforce automated complete shutdown outside office hours and on weekends to eliminate 24/7 "vampire" standby power draw.
  • Branch Hardware Modernization & Right-Sizing:
    • Replace aging 250W desktop towers with 30W energy-efficient mini PCs or laptops during scheduled refresh.
    • Deploys smart power strips to auto-cut power to peripherals when computers are shut down.

2. Target Area 2: Regional Data Center (Equipment & Cooling – Section 9.8 & Book 2)

  • Virtualization Consolidation:
    • Consolidate lightly loaded physical servers (running at 5%–15% capacity) into dense virtualized clusters (running at 70%–80% capacity).
    • Decommission and completely power down idle "zombie" servers running with zero active workloads.
  • Cooling Modernization (Hot/Cold Aisle Containment):
    • Install physical containment barriers to isolate cold server intake air from hot exhaust air.
    • Prevents thermal mixing, improving cooling chiller efficiency by 20%–30% and lowering facility Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) toward 1.15–1.2.

3. Target Area 3: Upstream Manufacturing & Grid Mix (Scope 2 & Scope 3 – Sections 9.7, 9.9 & 9.10)

  • Hardware Lifecycle Extension (Embodied Carbon Reduction – Section 9.7):
    • Extend server refresh cycles from 3 years to 5 years via modular CPU/RAM upgrades.
    • Directly addresses the upstream hardware manufacturing portion of the IT division's 30% emissions footprint.
  • Renewable Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs – Section 9.9):
    • Negotiate renewable energy utility contracts for the data center to shift from coal-heavy baseload grid electricity ($1.0\text{ kg CO}_2/\text{kWh}$) to clean solar/hydro/wind ($0\text{ kg CO}_2/\text{kWh}$).
  • Supply Chain Decarbonization Framework (Section 9.10):
    • 1. Cut at Home: Optimize internal branch PCs and server utilization first.
    • 2. Pocket Outsourcing Savings: Offload non-critical workloads to energy-efficient hyperscale cloud.
    • 3. Sweat the Supply Chain: Mandate upstream hardware vendors report verified carbon disclosures.
    • 4. Consider Non-Use: Prioritize software optimization over purchasing new hardware.
    • 5. Repeat & Improve: Audit quarterly energy savings against international standards.
💡 Exam Memorization Key: Break the answer into the 3 clear operational silos requested in the prompt: (1) Branch Offices (Power management & right-sizing), (2) Data Center (Virtualization & Hot/Cold aisle containment), and (3) Upstream & Grid (Lifecycle extension & Renewable PPAs).

Question 3: Actionable Daily Energy Use Steps to Reduce GHG Emissions

Spring 2026 – Q8 5 Marks Syllabus: Sections 9.8 & 9.1
Original Exam Question [Spring 2026, Question 8 – 5 Marks]

A company notices that its electricity bills and carbon footprint have been increasing due to heavy daily energy consumption in offices, including lighting, air conditioning, computers, and other electrical equipment. Management decides to adopt more sustainable energy practices and encourage employees to reduce environmental impact by changing everyday energy usage habits.

Explain the steps that can be taken in daily energy use by the company to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

Actionable Steps in Daily Energy Use to Reduce GHG Emissions (Section 9.8)

1. Computing & Office IT Equipment (Section 9.8)

  • Automated Sleep Policies (Tier 1):
    • Centrally configure operating systems across all office computers and monitors to enter low-power sleep mode after 15 minutes of user inactivity.
  • Mandatory Nightly and Weekend Shutdown:
    • Enforce an end-of-day shutdown policy for desktop workstations, monitors, copiers, and printers to completely eradicate vampire power consumption.
  • Batch Workload Scheduling:
    • Schedule heavy corporate data backups, virus definitions updates, and database indexing during off-peak nighttime grid hours.

2. Lighting & Environmental Controls (HVAC – Section 9.8)

  • Daylight Harvesting & Smart LED Fixtures:
    • Retrofit all fixtures with energy-efficient LED luminaires.
    • Install ambient light sensors that automatically dim artificial lighting when natural sunlight is sufficient.
    • Deploy Passive Infrared (PIR) occupancy sensors to automatically turn off lights in empty conference rooms, corridors, and restrooms.
  • Smart Thermostat Set-Points:
    • Moderate air conditioning set-points (set thermostat to 24°C–25°C during summer months); avoid wasteful overcooling in empty office wings.
    • Enforce automated temperature setbacks after standard working hours.

3. The Carbon Footprint Connection & Grid Fuel Mix (Section 9.1 & 9.8)

  • The Carbon Footprint Formula:
    Carbon Emissions (kg CO₂) = Energy Consumed (kWh) × Emission Factor (kg CO₂/kWh)
  • Why Conserving kWh Slashes Utility Plant Emissions:
    • Because utility grids rely heavily on fossil fuels, reducing daily kilowatt-hours directly avoids burning coal and gas at power plants.
Grid Power Source Carbon Intensity Profile Emissions Impact
Coal 1.0 kg CO₂ / kWh Highest emissions; dirtiest fossil fuel.
Oil / Diesel 0.8 kg CO₂ / kWh High emissions; used for emergency backup generators.
Natural Gas 0.4 kg CO₂ / kWh Moderate emissions (half of coal); methane leakage risk.
Hydroelectric Near-Zero Renewable clean electricity.
Solar & Wind 0.0 kg CO₂ / kWh Zero operational carbon footprint.

4. Fostering Employee Conservation Habits

  • Behavioral Changes:
    • Train employees to unplug mobile chargers and power bricks when not charging devices.
    • Close window blinds during hot sunny afternoons to reduce heat gain and relieve cooling loads.
    • Keep external doors and windows closed when air conditioning systems are running.
💡 Exam Memorization Key: Structure your answer into: 1. Computing IT equipment, 2. Lighting & HVAC, 3. Carbon footprint formula & fuel emission factors, and 4. Employee behavioral habits.

High-Yield Topic 1: Seven Practical Reasons to Adopt Green IT Regardless of Climate Beliefs

Exam Essential 5 Marks Potential Syllabus: Section 9.4
Common Exam Pattern

Explain the seven practical, commercial, and operational reasons why an organization should adopt Green Computing practices even if management or stakeholders are skeptical about climate change.

Seven Practical Reasons to Adopt Green Computing Regardless of Climate Beliefs (Section 9.4)

  1. 1. Financial Savings: Lower electricity utility bills and reduced mechanical cooling overhead directly boost the company's operating margin.
  2. 2. Business Risk Reduction: Protects the organization from fossil fuel price spikes, volatile utility tariffs, and anticipated carbon taxation.
  3. 3. Pollution Abatement: Cuts hazardous electronic waste, eliminates heavy metal landfill contamination (lead, mercury, cadmium), and reduces local toxic air pollution.
  4. 4. Worker Health & Welfare: Improves workplace ergonomics, minimizes toxic electronic component exposure, and enforces safe working standards in upstream supplier factories.
  5. 5. Talent & Customer Magnet: Attracts top-tier university engineering graduates and eco-conscious corporate enterprise clients who require green supplier certifications.
  6. 6. Regulatory Compliance: Proactively complies with mandatory environmental legislation (WEEE e-waste directives, RoHS substance bans, and building energy efficiency codes) before fines occur.
  7. 7. Positive Public Relations: Elevates corporate brand reputation and shields the company from investigative journalism, NGO protests, and consumer boycotts.

High-Yield Topic 2: The Five-Step Supply Chain Decarbonization Framework

Exam Essential 5 Marks Potential Syllabus: Section 9.10
Common Exam Pattern

Describe the Five-Step Supply Chain Decarbonization Framework used to systematically reduce greenhouse gas emissions across enterprise IT operations.

The Five-Step Supply Chain Decarbonization Framework (Section 9.10)

  1. 1. Cut at Home: Optimize internal operations first—eliminate idle "zombie" servers, consolidate physical machines, and enforce PC power management inside your own facilities.
  2. 2. Pocket Outsourcing Savings: Migrate non-core workloads to hyperscale cloud providers and energy-efficient shared co-location facilities that operate at industry-leading PUE metrics.
  3. 3. Sweat Your Supply Chain: Demand auditable carbon disclosures (Scope 3 accounting) from hardware manufacturers, OEMs, and component suppliers as a mandatory condition for procurement bids.
  4. 4. Consider Non-Use: Challenge whether new hardware purchases or physical business travel are necessary at all—prioritize software optimization, hardware reuse, and digital telepresence.
  5. 5. Repeat & Continuously Improve: Audit corporate energy and carbon savings annually against international sustainability benchmarks and iterate the cycle.

Complete Chapter 9 Lecture Notes Cheat Sheet

Sections 9.1 – 9.10
9.1 Why greenhouse gas emissions are important
⭐ PYQ Topic
  • Primary Atmospheric Greenhouse Gases (GHGs):
    • Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
    • Methane (CH₄)
    • Nitrous oxide (N₂O)
    • Fluorinated synthetic gases (HFCs, PFCs, SF₆)
  • IT Contribution: Computing drives emissions through the electricity required to operate hardware and the embodied energy consumed during manufacturing.
  • Energy vs. Emissions:
    • Energy Consumption: The quantity of kilowatt-hours (kWh) drawn from the electrical grid.
    • GHG Emissions: The mass of greenhouse gases released by power generation plants to produce that energy.
  • Carbon Footprint Formula:
    Carbon Emissions (kg CO₂) = Energy Consumed (kWh) × Emission Factor (kg CO₂/kWh)
9.2 Sources of greenhouse gases and warming

Natural Emission Sources:

  • Volcanic eruptions
  • Forest wildfires
  • Organic decomposition
  • Animal respiration & digestion

Anthropogenic (Human) Sources:

  • Coal Combustion: Primary fossil fuel burned for baseload electric grid power.
  • Petroleum (Refined Transportation Fuels):
    • Gasoline (petrol)
    • Diesel fuel
    • Aviation jet fuel
  • Natural Gas: Burned for industrial heating and peak electricity generation.
  • Hydraulic Fracturing (Fracking): Extraction process causing methane leakage into the atmosphere.
9.7 Reducing emissions I: embodied energy
⭐⭐⭐ Top PYQ Favorite

Embodied Energy Definition (Spring 2025 Q7 Exam Core):

  • The total cumulative non-renewable energy consumed across a product's pre-use lifecycle:
    • Raw material mining
    • Chemical refining
    • Semiconductor component fabrication
    • Factory assembly
    • Worldwide shipping and distribution

The Double Environmental Benefit:

  1. Lower Upfront Manufacturing Emissions: Less raw material extraction and fabrication carbon.
  2. Lower Operational Lifetime Emissions: Hardware engineered with lower physical material mass draws significantly less electricity throughout its operational lifespan.

Real-World Example (Device Classes): A modern tablet uses only ⅓ of the embodied energy of a desktop PC and emits 90% fewer operating emissions during daily use.

Strategies to Reduce Embodied Carbon:

  • Extend hardware refresh cycles from 3 years to 5–6 years.
  • Purchase certified refurbished and remanufactured hardware.
  • Deploy low-power thin clients and virtual desktops instead of bulky desktop towers.
9.8 Reducing emissions II: daily energy use (class-15)
⭐⭐⭐ Top PYQ Favorite

Operational Energy Reduction Strategies (Summer 2025 Q5 & Spring 2026 Q8):

  • Automated Power Management: Enforcing centralized sleep/standby policies across all client machines after 15 minutes of inactivity; auto-powering down monitors and peripheral devices.
  • Virtualization Consolidation: Consolidating lightly loaded physical servers to eliminate idle server electricity draw.
  • Off-Peak Workload Scheduling: Scheduling heavy data backups and batch computing during off-peak nighttime grid hours.

Carbon Intensity of Electrical Energy Sources:

  • Coal: 1.0 kg CO₂/kWh (highest carbon footprint; baseload fuel)
  • Oil / Diesel: 0.8 kg CO₂/kWh (reserved for emergency backup generators)
  • Natural Gas: 0.4 kg CO₂/kWh (half of coal; methane leak risk)
  • Hydroelectric: Near-zero operational carbon (renewable)
  • Solar & Wind: Zero operational carbon footprint (renewable)
9.9 Reducing emissions III: using different sources
⭐ PYQ Topic
  • Greening the Grid Mix: Shifting corporate power contracts from coal-heavy baseload utilities to renewable energy Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs).
  • On-Site Renewable Generation: Installing rooftop solar photovoltaic (PV) arrays on data center and office facilities.
  • Solar Thermal: Harnessing sunlight directly for facility water and building heating without electrical conversion losses.
  • Peak Shaving: Utilizing on-site battery storage during peak demand hours to avoid high utility peak tariffs.
